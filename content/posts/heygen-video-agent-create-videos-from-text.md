## What does HeyGen's Video Agent actually do?

The [Video Agent](https://www.heygen.com/) takes a text description and turns it into a complete, multi-scene video. You don't pick scenes manually. You don't drag clips around a timeline. You describe what you want — something like "a 30-second product explainer for my email marketing tool, friendly tone, with text overlays" — and the agent generates a full video plan.

It handles scene composition, pacing, avatar placement, background visuals, text overlays, background music, and captions. You can then edit any part of it in HeyGen's studio, but the initial draft is generated entirely by AI.

Think of it as the difference between writing an email from scratch and using an AI assistant that drafts it for you. You still review and tweak, but 80% of the work disappears.

## How is it different from standard HeyGen?

If you've used [HeyGen's standard workflow](/posts/heygen-talking-head-video-no-camera/), you know the process: write script → pick avatar → adjust settings → export. It's manual, but predictable. You control every word the avatar says.

The Video Agent flips this. Instead of controlling every detail, you describe the outcome. The AI decides how many scenes you need, what goes in each one, and how long each segment should be. Here's how they compare:

**Standard HeyGen** gives you maximum control. You write the exact script, pick the exact avatar, choose the background. Best for: polished talking-head videos where you already know what you want to say.

**Video Agent** gives you speed. You describe the video concept and let the AI build it. Best for: first drafts, content ideation, and videos where you're not sure about the structure yet.

I use both. For my regular talking-head content, I still write scripts manually. But when I need a quick product explainer or a social media video where the structure doesn't matter as much, the Video Agent saves me 30-45 minutes per video.

## How much does it cost in credits?

Here's where you need to pay attention. The Video Agent uses Avatar IV by default, which burns 20 credits per minute. On HeyGen's Creator plan ($29/month), you get 600 credits. That's roughly 30 minutes of Video Agent output — not 30 videos, but 30 total minutes.

A typical 60-second Video Agent creation uses about 20 credits. So you can make around 30 short videos per month on the Creator plan. Not bad, but not unlimited either.

On the free plan, you get 3 videos total (1 minute each), and the output is watermarked. Enough to test whether the feature works for you; not enough for real content production.

My recommendation: start with the free plan to see if the output quality meets your bar. If it does, Creator at $29/month is the sweet spot for solo creators. You can also use [Avatar III](/posts/heygen-avatar-v-vs-avatar-3/) for some videos (3 credits/minute) to stretch your budget further.

## What did I actually make with it?

I tested the Video Agent with three prompts to see how it handled different content types.

**Product explainer:** "A 30-second video explaining how my email marketing tool helps small businesses send better newsletters." The result was solid — three scenes, clean transitions, text overlays calling out key features. I changed one scene's background and adjusted the pacing, but the structure was good enough to use as-is.

**Social media clip:** "A 15-second Instagram Reel about why most people use ChatGPT wrong, casual tone." This one needed more editing. The AI picked a formal avatar and stiff pacing. I swapped the avatar and re-recorded with a more conversational delivery. Took about 10 minutes to fix.

**Tutorial intro:** "A 45-second intro for a tutorial about building your first automation with Make.com." The best result of the three. The agent nailed the pacing, picked appropriate visuals, and the text overlays were actually useful.

The pattern: the Video Agent works best when your prompt is specific about the audience and tone. Vague prompts produce generic results. Detailed prompts produce videos that feel hand-crafted.

## When should you skip it and use standard HeyGen?

Use the Video Agent when you need a first draft fast and plan to edit anyway, when the exact script doesn't matter much (social clips, explainers, intros), when you want to test video concepts before committing to full production, or when you're batch-creating similar videos and want a starting template.

Skip it when you have a word-perfect script, when the avatar's delivery matters (sales pitches, personal messages), when you need precise control over every transition and timing, or when the manual workflow already only takes you 10 minutes.

The Video Agent isn't a replacement for HeyGen's standard editor. It's an addition. Think of it as a brainstorming partner that also happens to render video.
