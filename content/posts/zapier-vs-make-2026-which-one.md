Two years ago, choosing between Zapier and Make was simple: Zapier was easier, Make was cheaper, and you picked based on your budget. In 2026 the decision is more complicated, and more interesting. Zapier has reinvented itself as an [AI orchestration platform](/posts/ai-agents-are-becoming-employees/), while Make has doubled down on being the best visual workflow builder on the market. They're no longer competing on the same axis. If you've been going back and forth between them, this is the breakdown I wish someone had given me.

I've used both extensively: Zapier for three years, Make for the last year. The answer to "which one?" depends entirely on what you're actually trying to build.

Here's the short version for anyone skimming: Zapier's free plan offers 100 tasks per month, Make's free tier provides 1,000 operations, and a 2024 Gartner report found small businesses adopting automation tools saved an average of 10 hours per week on repetitive tasks. Which platform you pick determines how much of that savings you keep after paying.

## What changed between Zapier and Make in 2026?

The biggest shift isn't pricing or features; it's philosophy.

**Zapier** went all-in on AI. They added Zapier MCP (Model Context Protocol), which lets AI agents like [Claude and ChatGPT trigger your automations directly](/posts/how-ai-calls-other-tools/). They launched Zapier Agents, Zapier Canvas, and unified their pricing so AI steps, code, and regular automations all use the same task-based model. The platform now positions itself as "AI orchestration."

**Make** stayed focused on visual workflow building. They added AI modules (you can call OpenAI, Claude, or Gemini within scenarios), but the core product is still the same drag-and-drop canvas that power users love. Make's bet is that most people don't need AI orchestration; they need reliable, affordable automation that works every time.

Neither approach is wrong. They're solving different problems.

## How do Zapier and Make pricing compare in 2026?

Make's free plan is 10x more generous: 1,000 operations per month with 2 active scenarios, versus Zapier's 100 tasks. Zapier's multi-step Zaps require the Professional plan starting at $19.99/month, while Make's Core plan unlocks unlimited scenarios and 1-minute intervals. I covered the [full Zapier pricing breakdown](/posts/zapier-pricing-2026-what-you-pay/) and a [deep dive on Make's pricing](/posts/make-com-pricing-2026-free-plan/) separately.

The quick math: if you're running 500+ automations per month, Make is significantly cheaper. If you're running 50 simple Zaps and want the easiest setup possible, Zapier's premium is worth the convenience.

Where it gets tricky: Zapier's AI steps can burn through tasks faster than regular automations. A single AI-powered workflow with three AI calls might use 6–10 tasks instead of 3. Make's credit system is more granular, which means more predictable costs — but also more math.

## Which is easier to learn, Zapier or Make?

Zapier is easier to start; Make is easier to scale.

**Zapier's builder** is linear. Trigger → action → action → done. If you can follow a recipe, you can build a Zap. I built my first working automation in under five minutes without watching a single tutorial. That matters when you're a [solo creator who just needs things to work](/posts/build-your-first-automation-in-15-minutes/).

**Make's builder** is visual and branching. You drag modules onto a canvas, connect them with lines, and add routers for conditional logic. It looks more complex because it is — but that complexity is power. Once you learn the interface (takes about an afternoon), you can build workflows Zapier can't even represent.

My rule of thumb: if your automation is "when X happens, do Y," use Zapier. If it's "when X happens, check A and B, then either do Y or Z depending on the condition, and also loop through these 50 items," use Make.

## Does Zapier or Make handle AI better?

Zapier wins on AI in 2026, and it's not close.

Zapier now supports MCP, which means AI models can directly invoke your automations. Imagine telling Claude "check my calendar and reschedule any meetings that conflict with my focus time" — and it actually does it, using your existing Zaps as building blocks. That's not hypothetical. It's live.

Zapier also has built-in AI steps that let you add ChatGPT or Claude calls directly into workflows without touching an external API. Need to summarize every incoming email before adding it to your CRM? One step, no code.

Make can do AI too — you can add HTTP modules to call any AI API — but it's not native. You're building the integration yourself. For a [non-technical user](/posts/what-is-ai-actually/), that's a meaningful gap. If your automations are purely "move data from A to B," the AI features don't matter and Make wins on price. If they involve AI decision-making, content generation, or agent-like behavior, Zapier is the clear pick right now.

## Which has better integrations, Zapier or Make?

Zapier claims 7,000+ integrations; Make claims 3,000+. Raw numbers favor Zapier, but the gap is misleading.

Most people use the same 50–100 apps: Google Workspace, Slack, Notion, Airtable, Stripe, Shopify, social media platforms, email marketing tools. Both platforms cover these completely. The long tail matters only if you're using niche or enterprise software.

Where Zapier genuinely wins: newer AI tools and SaaS products tend to launch Zapier integrations first. If you're an early adopter of [new AI tools](/posts/the-7-ai-tools-id-learn-first-if-i-started-over-in-2026/), Zapier will have the integration months before Make does.

Where Make wins: its integrations tend to be more granular. You get more actions per app, more filtering options, and more control over how data is transformed. Zapier's integrations are simpler but shallower.

## When should you choose Zapier over Make?

Choose Zapier if:

- You've never automated anything before and want the easiest possible start
- You want AI-powered workflows without touching an API
- You need integrations with the newest tools (especially AI products)
- You value speed of setup over cost efficiency
- You're building simple trigger → action workflows (under 5 steps)
- You want to use [Zapier MCP to connect AI agents](/posts/mcp-vs-skills-whats-the-difference/) to your tools

## When should you choose Make over Zapier?

Choose Make if:

- You're running 500+ automations per month and cost matters
- You need complex workflows with branching, loops, and error handling
- You prefer visual builders and want to see your entire workflow at a glance
- You're comfortable spending an afternoon learning the interface
- You want more control over data transformation between steps
- You don't need native AI integration in your automations

## What's the best way to start with automation?

Start with Zapier's free plan. Build 3–5 simple automations. Learn what triggers and actions mean. This is the same advice I gave in my [beginner comparison](/posts/make-vs-zapier-which-one-is-actually-easier/).

Then, when you hit Zapier's pricing ceiling — and you will, usually around the 100-task limit — evaluate whether to upgrade Zapier or migrate to Make. By that point you'll know enough about your own automation needs to make the right call.

Migration isn't painful. The concepts transfer directly: what Zapier calls a "Zap," Make calls a "scenario"; what Zapier calls a "task," Make calls an "operation." The logic is the same; the interface is different.

Zapier and Make have diverged enough in 2026 that they're not really direct competitors anymore. Zapier is building an AI automation platform. Make is building the best traditional automation tool on the market. Both are excellent at what they do.

If you want the easiest on-ramp and you're excited about [AI-powered workflows](/posts/stop-doing-things-manually-5-ai-workflows/), start with Zapier. If you want the best value for high-volume automation and you prefer visual control, start with Make. And if you're still not sure, try both free plans for a week — you'll know which one fits your brain by day three.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the main difference between Zapier and Make in 2026?**
Zapier focuses on AI orchestration, with features like MCP that let AI agents trigger your automations directly, plus built-in AI steps in workflows. Make emphasizes visual workflow building with branching, loops, and granular data control. In practice: Zapier is easier to start and better for AI-powered workflows; Make is cheaper at volume and better for complex, non-AI automations.

**Which is cheaper, Zapier or Make?**
Make. Its free plan gives 1,000 operations per month with 2 active scenarios, versus Zapier's 100 tasks. Make's Core plan unlocks unlimited scenarios and 1-minute intervals. If you run 500+ automations monthly, Make costs noticeably less. One caveat: Zapier's AI steps consume tasks faster (a workflow with three AI calls might use 6–10 tasks instead of 3), so estimate your usage before committing either way.

**Can I use Zapier and Make together?**
Yes. Many people run simple trigger → action workflows in Zapier and complex branching or looping workflows in Make, since both connect to overlapping apps like Google Workspace, Slack, Notion, Airtable, Stripe, and Shopify. There's no technical conflict; the main cost is maintaining two subscriptions and two sets of logic if you later consolidate.

**How hard is it to migrate from Zapier to Make?**
Not hard conceptually. The terminology changes (Zap becomes scenario, task becomes operation) but triggers, actions, filters, and logic all transfer. Rebuilding 3–5 simple automations takes an afternoon. The bigger adjustment is Make's visual canvas, which takes a few hours to feel comfortable in but then handles workflows Zapier can't represent, like conditional routers and loops over large data sets.

**Which platform should a complete beginner pick?**
Start with Zapier's free plan. Build 3–5 simple automations to learn triggers and actions, then reassess when you approach the 100-task monthly limit. By then you'll know whether your needs stay simple (stay on Zapier) or grow complex and high-volume (move to Make). Most beginners can judge which platform fits within a week of using both free plans.
