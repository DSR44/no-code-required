I've spent the last month testing every major AI agent designed to live inside your workspace: Salesforce's new Slackbot, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Gemini for Workspace. All three promise to be your tireless digital coworker, summarizing meetings and drafting emails while you sleep. But for solo builders and small teams, the real question is whether you can actually use them without an IT department and a six-figure budget. This is the practical **Agentforce vs Microsoft Copilot** breakdown you won't find in their marketing materials.

Here's the short version up front: Google Gemini comes included with Google Workspace Business plans at no extra cost, Microsoft Copilot costs $30 per user per month on top of your existing Microsoft 365 subscription, and Salesforce's Slackbot (rebuilt in 2026, powered by Claude) bundles its core agent features into Slack's paid plans. For a five-person team, that pricing difference means $1,800 a year versus zero additional spend, and a 2024 Forrester report on AI productivity tools found small businesses cite integration complexity as a bigger barrier than raw cost.

## Which AI agent is cheapest for a small team?

Google wins on price, and it's not close. If you're on a Google Workspace Business Standard plan or higher, Gemini is already included in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Meet. No add-on license, no new invoice. For a team already paying for Google Workspace, the AI features are essentially a free upgrade.

Microsoft is the expensive one. Copilot requires a $30/user/month license stacked on top of your existing Microsoft 365 subscription. For five people, that's $1,800 a year. Salesforce sits in the middle: basic Slackbot agent features come with Slack's paid plans, but advanced actions that connect to external apps or run deep CRM automation may require Salesforce licenses, which get complicated (and pricey) for a tiny team.

## Which one can you set up without an IT department?

Gemini and Slackbot, easily. You activate Gemini with a single admin toggle, and Slackbot's core features work as soon as you connect your Slack workspace. I timed myself: under five minutes for both.

Copilot is the friction point. It works best when your files live in OneDrive or SharePoint and your communications run through Teams and Outlook. If your team uses mixed tools (say, Gmail for email but Word for documents), you'll either migrate your workflow into Microsoft's ecosystem or accept a watered-down experience. That's the same integration complexity the Forrester report flagged as the top barrier for small businesses, and it's a real workflow shift, not a checkbox.

## What can each agent actually do?

All three summarize meetings, draft emails, and handle the grunt work, but they live in different places and connect to different data.

**Salesforce Slackbot (powered by Claude)** was rebuilt from the ground up in 2026 and is now a genuine AI agent inside Slack. It summarizes threads, searches across connected apps, drafts replies, and takes actions in Salesforce CRM. I covered [how to use it in detail](/posts/salesforce-slackbot-ai-agent-guide/); the short version is that it's the most accessible of the three for non-technical users. If your team lives in Slack, it's the lowest-friction option.

**Microsoft Copilot** is the most deeply embedded agent in a productivity suite. It sits inside Teams, Outlook, Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, so it can summarize meetings, draft emails, analyze spreadsheets, and generate presentations without you leaving the app. The trade-off is that its power depends on your whole workflow living inside Microsoft's ecosystem.

**Google Gemini for Workspace** lives in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Meet. It's newer than the other two and still catching up on features, but it handles the core jobs (drafting, summarizing, spreadsheet analysis) well enough for most small-team work.

## Which one understands your actual work?

This is where context matters more than features. A generic summary of a meeting you didn't attend is nice. An agent that can see your CRM data, your project threads, and your actual files is useful.

Slackbot has the edge if your work context lives in Slack conversations and Salesforce records, because it searches across connected apps and acts on CRM data directly. Copilot is strongest when your history and documents are inside Microsoft 365; it knows your email threads and your spreadsheets because it's sitting on top of them. Gemini is the weakest on deep context right now, though it's improving with each Workspace update, and for most Gmail-and-Docs teams it already knows enough to be genuinely helpful.

## Which should a solo builder pick?

Pick based on where your work already lives, not on feature lists. If you pay for Google Workspace, turn on Gemini and spend the $0. If your team runs on Slack and you touch Salesforce, Slackbot is the obvious fit. Copilot is worth the $30/user/month only if you're fully committed to Microsoft 365 and you'll actually use it across Word, Excel, and Teams daily. For a mixed-tools team of one to ten people, I'd start with Gemini, test Slackbot if you're Slack-centric, and treat Copilot as a later upgrade once revenue justifies the license cost.

## FAQs

**How much does Microsoft Copilot cost compared to Google Gemini?**
Copilot costs $30 per user per month on top of your existing Microsoft 365 subscription, which is $1,800 per year for a five-person team. Gemini is included at no extra cost with Google Workspace Business Standard plans and higher, making it the cheapest option for teams already on Google.

**Can I use Salesforce's Slackbot AI agent without buying Salesforce?**
Yes, for the basics. Core agent features like thread summaries, drafting, and cross-app search are part of Slack's paid plans. However, advanced actions that connect to external apps or automate Salesforce CRM workflows may require Salesforce licenses, which add cost and complexity for small teams.

**Which AI agent is easiest to set up for a non-technical team?**
Google Gemini and Salesforce Slackbot are the easiest. Gemini activates with a single admin toggle in Google Workspace, and Slackbot works as soon as you connect your Slack workspace. Copilot typically requires your files in OneDrive/SharePoint and communications in Teams/Outlook to deliver full value.

**Is Copilot worth $30/month for a small business?**
Only if your workflow already lives entirely inside Microsoft 365 and you'll use it daily across Word, Excel, Teams, and Outlook. A 2024 Forrester report found small businesses cite integration complexity as a bigger barrier than cost, and Copilot's value drops sharply when your tools are mixed. Start with a cheaper option first.

**What did Salesforce change about Slackbot in 2026?**
Salesforce rebuilt Slackbot from the ground up in 2026, turning it from a simple assistant into a genuine AI agent powered by Claude. It now summarizes threads, searches across connected apps, drafts replies, and takes actions directly in Salesforce CRM, all from inside Slack.
