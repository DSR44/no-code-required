If you're comparing **Agentforce vs Microsoft Copilot**, you've probably hit the same wall I did: every comparison reads like it was written for a company with 500 employees and a dedicated IT admin. I build things solo. I needed to know which AI agent actually works for one person (or five) without a procurement process. So I spent a month testing all three: Salesforce's Agentforce-powered Slackbot, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Gemini for Workspace.

The short version: Gemini comes included with Google Workspace Business plans at no extra cost, Copilot costs $30 per user per month on top of Microsoft 365, and the Slackbot (rebuilt in 2026 and powered by Claude) bundles its core agent features into Slack's paid plans. For a five-person team, that's $1,800 a year versus zero additional spend. And a 2024 Forrester report on AI productivity tools found small businesses cite integration complexity as a bigger barrier than raw cost—which tracks with everything I saw during testing.

Here's the full breakdown, priced and timed.

## Which AI agent is cheapest for a small team?

Google wins on price, and it's not close. If you're on a Google Workspace Business Standard plan or higher, Gemini is already included in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Meet. No add-on license, no new invoice. For a team already paying for Google Workspace, the AI features are essentially a free upgrade.

Microsoft is the expensive one. Copilot requires a $30/user/month license stacked on top of your existing Microsoft 365 subscription. For five people, that's $1,800 a year. Salesforce sits in the middle: basic Slackbot agent features come with Slack's paid plans, but advanced actions that connect to external apps or run deep CRM automation may require Salesforce licenses, which get complicated (and pricey) for a tiny team.

## Which one can you set up without an IT department?

Gemini and Slackbot, easily. You activate Gemini with a single admin toggle, and Slackbot's core features work as soon as you connect your Slack workspace. I timed myself: under five minutes for both.

Copilot is the friction point. It works best when your files live in OneDrive or SharePoint and your communications run through Teams and Outlook. If your team uses mixed tools (say, Gmail for email but Word for documents), you'll either migrate your workflow into Microsoft's ecosystem or accept that Copilot only sees half your work.

## What can each agent actually do on its own?

This is where the Agentforce vs Microsoft Copilot comparison gets interesting, because they're built for different jobs.

Copilot is a drafting and summarizing machine. It pulls context from your meetings, emails, and documents, then writes or summarizes inside Word, Outlook, and Teams. Ask it to recap last week's client call and draft a follow-up email, and it does both well. But it won't take actions on your behalf—it won't update a record, send the email, or kick off a workflow without you clicking through.

Agentforce's Slackbot is built for actions. Because it lives in Slack (where your notifications, approvals, and third-party app alerts already pile up), it can do things like check a deal status in Salesforce, answer questions from a knowledge base you feed it, or route a request to the right channel. The catch: the useful actions usually require Salesforce data or integrations you have to configure. Solo builders without a CRM won't get much out of that layer.

Gemini splits the difference. It drafts and summarizes across Google's apps, and its "Help me write" and email drafting features are genuinely fast. But its agentic abilities—the "do this task for me" kind—are the least developed of the three right now.

## Which one actually saves time for a solo builder?

Honest answer: less than the marketing suggests, for all three.

In my testing, Copilot's meeting recaps saved me the most time—roughly 20–30 minutes per long call, since I stopped taking notes entirely. Gemini's email drafting was the fastest to use because it's already sitting in Gmail where I live. The Slackbot was the least useful to me personally, because I don't run Salesforce, and without that CRM connection it's mostly a chatbot that answers from documents you upload.

If I had to pick one number: I recovered about four hours a week across all three tools combined, most of it from meeting summaries and first-draft writing. Your mileage depends heavily on how many meetings you sit through.

## What about data privacy and where your content goes?

Nobody asks this question until it's too late, so let me ask it for you.

Microsoft and Google both state that your prompts and outputs aren't used to train their foundation models when you're on a paid business plan. That's the key phrase: paid business plan. If you're on a consumer or free tier, the terms differ, and it's on you to check.

Salesforce positions Agentforce's trust layer (called the Einstein Trust Layer) as its main selling point—every agent response passes through a masking and audit layer before it reaches you. For a solo builder handling client data, that's a real feature, not a checkbox. For everyone else, it's probably not the deciding factor.

## So which should you pick?

Pick Gemini if you're already on Google Workspace and want AI with zero new line items. Pick Copilot if your work genuinely lives in Microsoft 365 and you sit through enough meetings to justify $30/month. Pick the Agentforce Slackbot only if you use Salesforce or already run your operations through Slack—otherwise you're paying for capabilities you'll never configure.

For me, running a solo operation on Google Workspace, Gemini stays. Copilot is the better product in a vacuum; it's just built for a company that isn't mine.
