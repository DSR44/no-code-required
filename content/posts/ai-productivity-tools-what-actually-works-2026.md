Everyone's selling you an AI tool in 2026. Every newsletter, every podcast, every X thread: "you NEED this in your stack." I've tested more of these than I'd like to admit, and most of them are solving problems you don't have.

The useful question changed sometime last year. It used to be "can it do something cool?" Now it's "does it actually save me time?" Those are different questions. A tool that generates impressive demos but requires 45 minutes of prompt engineering to get a usable output isn't productivity; it's a hobby. By my own accounting, the five tools that survived my 2026 cull cost $65-100/month combined and save me one to two hours a day. Everything else got cancelled.

I've written about [which AI tools I actually use every day](/posts/the-tools-i-actually-use-every-day/) and how to [escape AI tool overwhelm](/posts/ai-tool-overwhelm-how-to-escape/). This post is the 2026 update — what's changed, what's gotten better, and what I stopped using entirely.

## Which AI tools are actually worth paying for in 2026?

Five, in my case: Claude, Perplexity, Make.com, Notion AI, and ElevenLabs. Each one earns its subscription by saving measurable hours per week, and all five have free tiers you can test before paying anything.

**Claude (Anthropic)** — If I had to pick one AI tool for 2026, this is it. Claude handles long documents, keeps context across complex tasks, and the writing quality keeps improving. Since [Claude Sonnet 5 launched](/posts/claude-sonnet-5-agents-solo-builders/), the cost-performance ratio is hard to beat. I use it for research synthesis, first drafts, contract review, and workflow planning. The 1M token context window means I can upload entire project briefs and get coherent output. At $20/month for Pro, it's the best ROI in my stack. [claude.ai](https://claude.ai)

**Perplexity AI** — This replaced Google for 80% of my research. Instead of wading through SEO-optimized blog posts, I get cited answers in seconds. Pro search mode is genuinely good at finding recent data, comparing sources, and flagging when something is uncertain. Free tier is generous; Pro at $20/month is worth it if research is part of your daily work. [perplexity.ai](https://perplexity.ai)

**Make.com** — Still the best automation platform for non-coders. I've covered [building your first automation in 15 minutes](/posts/build-your-first-automation-in-15-minutes/), and Make continues to be the backbone. What changed in 2026: their AI module now lets you add Claude or GPT calls directly in workflows without API keys. If you're running any kind of [client follow-up automation](/posts/automate-client-follow-ups-no-code/), this is the tool. Free tier handles most solo builder needs. [make.com](https://make.com)

**Notion AI** — If you already use Notion, the AI add-on at $10/month is a no-brainer. It summarizes meeting notes, drafts project briefs, and answers questions about your workspace. If you don't use Notion, don't start just for the AI — it's not better than standalone tools. But for existing users, it kills the copy-paste dance between your notes and ChatGPT. [notion.so](https://notion.so)

**ElevenLabs** — Voice generation has gotten eerily good. I use it for [blog audio narration](/posts/the-tools-i-actually-use-every-day/) and content production, and for most use cases you can't tell it apart from a human recording. At $5/month for the starter plan, it's absurdly cheap. [elevenlabs.io](https://elevenlabs.io)

## Which AI tools look impressive but don't save time?

The recurring offenders: dedicated AI writing generators, image generators used for everyday content, and "all-in-one" AI platforms. All three share the same flaw — the time they demand exceeds the time they return.

**AI writing generators (Jasper, Writesonic, etc.)** — I know this is controversial. These tools generate text fast, but the output almost always needs heavy editing, often more editing than writing from scratch with a good Claude or ChatGPT prompt. A well-structured prompt to Claude with your brand guidelines uploaded beats any dedicated AI writing tool. The $49-99/month price tags don't justify the marginal improvement over a $20/month Claude Pro subscription. We covered this in [I tested 10 AI writing tools](/posts/tested-10-ai-writing-tools/), and the conclusion hasn't changed.

**AI image generators for daily content** — [Midjourney](/posts/ai-images-which-tool-actually-works/) produces remarkable images. For daily production, though — blog covers, social posts, thumbnails — the time spent crafting prompts, iterating on variations, and upscaling often exceeds the time saved. Canva's AI features handle 90% of daily design needs faster. Midjourney is for when you need something extraordinary, not your Tuesday blog post.

**"All-in-one" AI platforms** — Every month someone launches a platform promising to replace ChatGPT + Notion + Make + your email client. Every month I try one. Every month I go back to my existing stack. These tools aren't bad; they're mediocre at everything while being great at nothing. Specialized tools win because they focus on one workflow and nail it.

## What does a real AI stack cost per month?

$65-100/month for the stack I described. Here's the breakdown the "10 AI tools you NEED" articles skip:

- Claude Pro: $20/month
- Perplexity Pro: $20/month
- Make.com: $10-30/month depending on operations
- Notion AI: $10/month add-on
- ElevenLabs: $5-22/month

Add image generation, video tools, or specialized apps and you're easily at $150-200/month.

The smart move: start with free tiers. Every tool listed has one. Use free Claude, free Perplexity, free Make.com, and free Notion for a month and see which ones you actually reach for daily. Upgrade only those. Don't pay for tools you "might use eventually" — that's how subscription graveyards happen. I covered the [AI subscription price war](/posts/ai-subscription-price-war-what-to-pay-for/) earlier this year, and the advice holds: pay for the tool that saves you the most hours per week, not the one with the most features.

## What workflow actually saves time with AI tools?

The compounding one, where each tool hands off to the next. Individually, each step saves 15-30 minutes; connected, the chain saves me 1-2 hours daily. Here's what I run:

1. **Research** in Perplexity → export key findings
2. **Draft** in Claude with findings + brand context → first draft
3. **Automate** distribution in Make.com → schedule across platforms
4. **Track** in Notion → update project status automatically

If you're building an [AI agent workflow](/posts/ai-agents-becoming-employees-solo-business/), this is the foundation. The tools don't replace your judgment; they handle the mechanical parts so you can focus on the decisions that matter.

## What AI tools did I stop using in 2026?

Grammarly, Zapier, and dedicated AI social media tools. Each got cut for the same reason: something I already pay for does the job as well or better.

**Grammarly** — Claude and ChatGPT handle editing better, and they understand context. Grammarly's AI suggestions have improved, but not enough to justify a separate subscription when I'm already paying for a general-purpose AI.

**Zapier** — Make.com does the same thing at half the price with a more visual interface. If you're already deep in Zapier's ecosystem, switching costs aren't worth it. For new automations, start with Make.

**Dedicated AI social media tools** — Buffer, Hootsuite, and their AI features are fine. But a Make.com automation that feeds your content from Notion to your scheduling tool does the same thing without the $30/month subscription. We covered this with [aitoearn](/posts/aitoearn-free-scheduling-tool-pays-you-to-post/) — free tools exist that do the job.

## What makes an AI productivity tool actually work?

Three traits, and every tool on my "earned their place" list has them: it integrates with tools you already use, it saves measurable time (not hypothetical time), and it doesn't require a PhD in prompt engineering to get usable output.

Start small. Pick one tool from the list above, use it daily for two weeks, and if you can't point to specific hours saved, drop it and try another. The goal isn't the most impressive AI stack; it's the one that makes your work measurably faster.

And if someone tells you that you absolutely NEED their $200/month AI platform to stay competitive? They're selling you fear, not productivity.

## FAQ

**How much should I spend on AI tools in 2026?**
A functional stack of five tools — Claude Pro, Perplexity Pro, Make.com, Notion AI, and ElevenLabs — runs $65-100/month. Start with free tiers for a month, identify which tools you use daily, and only upgrade those. Adding image and video tools can push you to $150-200/month, so upgrade deliberately.

**Is Claude Pro worth $20/month?**
In my experience, yes — it's the highest-ROI tool in my stack. The 1M token context window handles entire project briefs, and after Claude Sonnet 5's launch the cost-performance ratio is hard to beat. I use it for research synthesis, drafts, contract review, and workflow planning.

**Are AI writing tools like Jasper worth it?**
Usually not. Their output typically needs more editing than writing from scratch with a well-structured Claude or ChatGPT prompt that includes your brand guidelines. At $49-99/month versus Claude Pro's $20/month, the marginal improvement rarely justifies the price.

**Should I use Zapier or Make.com for automation?**
For new automations, start with Make.com — it does the same work at roughly half the price with a more visual interface, and its 2026 AI module lets you add Claude or GPT calls without API keys. If you're already deep in Zapier's ecosystem, the switching cost probably isn't worth it.

**What's the fastest way to test whether an AI tool is worth keeping?**
Use it daily for two weeks and track whether you can point to specific hours saved. If you can't name concrete time savings, cancel it. Tools that generate impressive demos but need 45 minutes of prompt engineering per usable output are hobbies, not productivity.
