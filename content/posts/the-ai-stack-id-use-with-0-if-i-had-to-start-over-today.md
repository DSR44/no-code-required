If I lost everything tomorrow — no subscriptions, no bookmarks, no muscle memory — and had to rebuild an AI workflow from zero dollars, here's what I'd set up before lunch. Not the "best 47 tools" list that makes your eyes glaze over. Six tools that form a working system, and every one has a free tier that's genuinely usable rather than a "free trial then $49/month" trap.

Here's the number that matters: the six tools below cost $0 combined, and one of them alone (Make.com) gives you 1,000 free automation operations per month, enough for hundreds of automated workflows. I tested dozens of tools over the past year; these six are the ones that survived because they do real work, connect to each other, and don't charge you for existing.

## What's the best free AI stack for a solopreneur with no budget?

Six tools: ChatGPT for drafting and brainstorming, Claude for writing that sounds human, Canva for visuals, Make.com for automation, Notion for organization, and Perplexity for research. Each has a free tier that's usable from day one, and together they cover research, writing, design, distribution, and tracking. I'd rather run six connected free tools than one paid suite, because each one is best-in-class at its job and they hand work off cleanly.

## Which AI should you start with: ChatGPT or Claude?

Start with ChatGPT, add Claude when ChatGPT's output starts feeling generic. The free tier of [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com) gives you GPT-4o mini, which handles 90% of what a solopreneur needs: drafting emails, brainstorming content ideas, writing product descriptions, summarizing research, explaining concepts you don't understand yet. The paid tier is $20/month for GPT-4o, image generation, and advanced data analysis, but you can start without any of that.

[Claude](https://claude.ai) by Anthropic, on its free tier, gives you Claude 3.5 Sonnet, which is stronger at long-form writing and less likely to open with the "As an AI language model" preface that makes readers tune out. My split: ChatGPT for brainstorming, outlines, and quick tasks; Claude for anything that needs to sound like a person wrote it — blog posts, newsletters, proposals. Claude writes like someone who reads books. ChatGPT writes like someone who reads the internet.

The real reason ChatGPT anchors a $0 stack has nothing to do with the chat interface. It teaches you how to write clear prompts, and every other tool on this list gets more useful once you have that skill. Spend your first 30 minutes asking it to help with something real — a sales email, a competitor analysis. Not "tell me about AI." You'll learn more in that half hour than from any course. If you want a structured starting point, check out [the one prompt that changed everything](/posts/the-one-prompt-that-changed-everything/). Torn between the two tools? I compared them directly in [ChatGPT vs. Claude](/posts/chatgpt-alternatives-2026-actually-worth-switching/).

## How do you create professional visuals for free?

Use [Canva](https://canva.com). You need visuals for social posts, presentations, lead magnets, blog graphics, and unless you're a designer, you're not going to learn Photoshop — nor should you have to. Canva's free tier includes thousands of templates, a drag-and-drop editor, and enough AI features (background remover, magic resize, text-to-image) to make you look like you know what you're doing. The $13/month Pro tier adds brand kits and more templates, but the free version handles 80% of what a solo business needs.

The power move is pairing Canva with ChatGPT: ask ChatGPT to write your social captions, then build the graphics in Canva. You just automated your content pipeline without touching an automation tool. I walk through that workflow in [how I built a blog in 1 hour with AI](/posts/how-i-built-a-blog-in-1-hour-with-ai/).

## How much free automation do you actually get?

Make.com's free tier gives you 1,000 operations per month, and since most automations use 1–3 operations each, that's hundreds of automated workflow runs every month for nothing. [Make.com](https://make.com) (formerly Integromat) connects your tools visually — "when I get a new email, summarize it with AI and add it to my Notion database" — with drag-and-drop blocks and no code.

What I actually use it for: auto-posting blog content to social media, saving important emails to my task manager, monitoring competitor websites for changes, and generating weekly reports from multiple data sources.

Never touched automation? Start with [build your first automation in 15 minutes](/posts/build-your-first-automation-in-15-minutes/), which walks through a real workflow step by step. And if you're weighing Make against Zapier, I compared them head-to-head in [Make vs. Zapier: which one is actually easier](/posts/make-vs-zapier-which-one-is-actually-easier/).

## Do you need Notion AI if you already have ChatGPT?

No. The [Notion](https://notion.so) free tier gives you unlimited pages and blocks for personal use — plenty for a content calendar, client tracker, project dashboard, or knowledge base — and the $10/month AI add-on is redundant when you already have ChatGPT and Claude for drafting and summarizing. Skip it for now.

What makes Notion worth including is how well it plays with Make.com. You can automate data into Notion from almost anywhere: emails, form submissions, API responses, RSS feeds. Your second brain fills itself while you sleep.

I've tried every productivity tool out there and Notion is the one that stuck, mostly because it's as simple or as complex as you make it. Start with a blank page and add structure as you go. The biggest beginner mistake is spending three days building the "perfect" system instead of using it — I covered that in [the mistakes I made so you don't have to](/posts/the-mistakes-i-made-so-you-dont-have-to/).

## Where do you get research you can actually trust for free?

Perplexity. Google results are full of ads and SEO-gamed articles; when you need real answers — competitor research, market analysis, technical explanations — [Perplexity](https://perplexity.ai) does the job better. The free tier gives you unlimited basic searches plus a handful of Pro searches per day, and Pro searches are the ones that read multiple sources, synthesize them, and return a cited answer. It's like a research assistant who works free and doesn't hallucinate (much).

Typical queries I run: competitor analysis ("what tools does [company] use?"), market research ("what's the average conversion rate for online coaches?"), technical explanations ("how does [API] work in plain English?"), and fact-checking AI output before I publish it. Then feed the findings into ChatGPT or Claude for analysis and writing. That's a research-to-content pipeline costing $0.

## How do the six tools connect in one workflow?

In practice it looks like this: research a topic in Perplexity, draft the content in Claude (or use ChatGPT for outlines), design the visuals in Canva, organize everything in Notion, then automate distribution with Make.com — auto-post to social, auto-send the newsletter. Finally, use ChatGPT to analyze what's working and decide what to try next.

Six tools, zero dollars, and a complete loop from idea to published content to automated distribution. You can run a real business on this for months before upgrading anything.

## What should you pay for first when money comes in?

In this order: **ChatGPT Plus ($20/month)**, because the jump from GPT-4o mini to GPT-4o pays off once you're using AI daily; then **Make.com Standard ($10/month)**, whose 10,000 operations unlock serious automation; then **Notion AI ($10/month)** once you're living in Notion; and finally **Claude Pro ($20/month)** if long-form writing is core to your business.

None of these are urgent, though. The free tiers are genuinely good enough to start, build, and validate your business. Anyone telling you that you need to spend $200/month on tools before you've made your first dollar is selling you something.

You don't need a budget. You need six bookmarks and a willingness to experiment. These aren't the cheapest or trendiest options — they're the ones that actually work together as a system. Start with ChatGPT, add the others as needed, and upgrade only when a free tier is genuinely holding you back.

## FAQ

**Is a free AI tool stack really enough to run a business?**
Yes, for the first several months at least. ChatGPT's free tier covers most drafting and brainstorming, Canva's free plan handles 80% of a solo business's design needs, and Make.com's 1,000 monthly operations support hundreds of automation runs. Upgrade only when a specific limit — like Make's operation count — actually blocks revenue work.

**What's the difference between ChatGPT and Claude for a solopreneur?**
ChatGPT (free tier, GPT-4o mini) is the better all-rounder for brainstorming, outlining, and quick tasks. Claude (free tier, 3.5 Sonnet) produces stronger long-form writing that sounds more human, making it the better choice for blog posts, newsletters, and proposals. Most people benefit from both.

**How many free automations can I run on Make.com?**
The free plan includes 1,000 operations per month. Since a typical workflow uses 1–3 operations per run, that translates to hundreds of runs monthly — enough for auto-posting to social media, saving emails to your task manager, and generating weekly reports.

**Should I pay for Notion AI?**
Not at the start. Notion's free tier offers unlimited pages and blocks, and the $10/month AI add-on duplicates what ChatGPT and Claude already do for free. It becomes worth considering only after Notion becomes your daily workspace.

**What's the right order to upgrade to paid plans?**
ChatGPT Plus first ($20/month), then Make.com Standard ($10/month), then Notion AI ($10/month), then Claude Pro ($20/month) if long-form writing drives your revenue. Each upgrade should follow real usage, not fear of missing out.
