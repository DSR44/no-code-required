The free plan is legitimately useful — not a 14-day trial, not a crippled demo. You get 1,000 operations per month, 2 active scenarios, a 15-minute minimum interval between runs, and 512MB of data transfer. For a solo creator automating a few tasks — like syncing form responses to Google Sheets or posting to social media — this is plenty. I ran on it for three months before I needed more. But here's the nuance the official page misses: those 1,000 operations disappear fast if you're using AI tools, which most modern automations now do. A single ChatGPT-4 step in your workflow can consume 3-5 operations instead of one.

## How AI Integrations Are Changing Make.com Credit Calculations in 2026

This is the angle most pricing articles miss. According to a 2026 automation efficiency report, 65% of professional Make.com workflows now include at least one AI step — up from just 20% in 2024. What does this mean for the **make pricing official 2026** structure? It means your 1,000 free operations might last two weeks instead of a month.

Let me break it down practically. A standard "email to spreadsheet" automation uses 1 operation per email. Simple. But if you add an AI summarization step before logging it, that's suddenly 3 operations per trigger — one to receive the email, one to send it to OpenAI, and one to write the summary. Suddenly, your 1,000 operations only cover 333 emails instead of 1,000. This is why Make.com's **free plan official operations** limit hits faster than you'd expect in 2026.

I learned this the hard way when I added an AI classifier to sort customer support tickets. My operation usage tripled overnight. The fix wasn't just upgrading — it was optimizing. I switched from GPT-4 to GPT-3.5 for simple classification tasks, which uses 1 operation instead of 2. I also learned to use Make.com's "set variable" modules to cache AI responses, so the same query doesn't cost operations twice. These tweaks let me stay on the free plan for two more months.

## The Core plan: where most people land

The Core plan is where Make.com starts to feel like a real platform. You get unlimited active scenarios, 1-minute minimum intervals (vs. 15 minutes on free), 40-minute maximum execution time, 5GB of data transfer, and 30-day log history. You also unlock custom apps, scenario inputs and outputs, and custom functions with JavaScript.

The jump from 2 active scenarios to unlimited is the big one. On the free plan, you have to deactivate one scenario to activate another once you hit the limit. On Core, you can have as many running as you need. The 1-minute interval also matters if you're building anything time-sensitive — customer message automation, order processing, or real-time data syncs. I upgraded when I realized I was manually checking my automations every hour because the 15-minute delay meant I was always 14 minutes behind my competitors.

## When you actually need the Pro plan

This is where most guides fail. They say "Pro is for businesses" without explaining what that actually means. In practice, you need Pro when you're running operations that touch multiple external services per trigger — especially AI APIs, databases, and error-handling workflows. The Pro plan's 10GB data transfer and 200MB maximum file size handle larger payloads, but the real value is in the technical features: scenario debugging tools, data stores, and team collaboration options.

The **make.com free plan** is perfect for learning and simple automations. But once you're processing more than 500 AI-enhanced workflows per month, or you need reliability for customer-facing processes, Core is your floor. Pro is for when you're building internal tools or client-facing automations where debugging and data integrity matter more than cost.
