I was scrolling through a blog I hadn't touched in three months when I realized something annoying: I had 47 published posts, each containing research, quotes, frameworks, and insights — and I was still starting from scratch every time I needed a tweet, an email, or a reel script. The content was already there. I just wasn't using it.

If you've been blogging for any length of time, you're sitting on a library of content that can feed your social channels, your email list, and your video pipeline for weeks without writing a single new word. The problem isn't creating more content. It's extracting what you've already created.

## How one blog post becomes 15+ pieces

Let me walk through a real example. Say you published a 1,200-word post about [building your first automation](/posts/build-your-first-automation-in-15-minutes/). Here's what's hiding inside it:

**From the title:** A tweet-sized hook. "I automated my client follow-ups in 15 minutes. Here's the exact setup." That's one post.

**From the introduction:** The problem statement — your pain point, the friction you felt, why you wrote the post. That's a standalone tweet or LinkedIn post. People connect with the problem more than the solution.

**From each H2 section:** A single tip that works as a standalone social post. If your post has five sections, that's five tweets, five LinkedIn posts, or five Instagram carousel slides. Each one links back to the full post.

**From the data or quotes you cited:** An infographic or data visualisation. Pull the stat, add one sentence of context, and you have a Pinterest pin, a Twitter image post, or a Story slide. I covered [AI images that actually work](/posts/ai-images-which-tool-actually-works/) — use them.

**From the conclusion:** An email to your list. "I wrote about X this week, and the biggest takeaway was Y. Here's why it matters for you." One paragraph, one link, done.

**From the full post:** A video script. Read the post out loud, edit for spoken language, and you have a 3–5 minute YouTube video or reel. I covered [how to build branded reels](/posts/heygen-talking-head-video-no-camera/) from existing content.

One post. Fifteen pieces. And you didn't have to research anything new.

## The extraction workflow

Here's the actual process I use. It takes about 20 minutes per post and produces a week of content.

**Step 1: Pull the skeleton.** Open your blog post. Copy every H2 heading and the first sentence under each one. That's your content outline. Each H2 becomes a standalone post.

**Step 2: Find the quotable lines.** Scan for sentences that make a point in under 280 characters. If you can't find any, your post might be too academic — but usually there are 3–5 lines that work as tweets. Pull them into a document.

**Step 3: Extract the data.** Any statistic, study reference, or specific number becomes a data visualisation. Tools like [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) or [VistaCreate](https://create.vista.com/) let you build branded data cards in minutes. I covered [the tools I actually use](/posts/the-tools-i-actually-use-every-day/) — Canva is in there for a reason.

**Step 4: Write the email summary.** Three sentences: what I wrote, why it matters, link to read more. Your email list doesn't need the full post — they need the insight and the link.

**Step 5: Record the video.** Use the post as a script. [HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/) or a simple screen recording works. The post already has the structure — intro, body, conclusion. You're just translating it to video. I covered [video workflows for solopreneurs](/posts/heygen-batch-video-content-workflow/) if you want the full pipeline.

**Step 6: Schedule everything.** Use [Buffer](https://buffer.com/), [Hootsuite](https://hootsuite.com/), or [Later](https://later.com/) to drip the content over the next 7–14 days. One post becomes a week of social activity without you touching it again.

## Why this works for solo builders

If you're running a [solo business](/posts/can-you-make-10k-month-ai-automations/), content creation is probably your biggest time sink. You know you should be posting daily on social media, sending weekly emails, and publishing videos — but you also know you have a business to run.

Repurposing solves the time problem. Instead of creating 15 new pieces of content per week, you create one blog post and extract 15 pieces from it. The research is already done. The writing is already done. You're just reshaping it for different formats.

This is exactly what [AI agents are good at](/posts/ai-agents-are-becoming-employees/). Feed a blog post to Claude or ChatGPT and ask: "Give me 5 tweet variations, 3 LinkedIn posts, and an email summary from this post." You'll get usable drafts in 30 seconds. Edit for your voice, schedule, move on.

## The content calendar trick

Most solo builders don't have a content calendar because it feels like more work. Here's the cheat: your blog archive IS your content calendar.

Open your site. List every post. Assign each one a week. For post #1, extract tweets for Monday, a LinkedIn post for Wednesday, an email for Friday, and a reel script for the following Monday. Move to post #2. Repeat.

In 30 minutes, you have a month of content mapped out. No brainstorming. No "what should I post today?" anxiety. The content already exists — you're just distributing it.

I wrote about [groupthink in AI](/posts/ai-groupthink-problem-solo-builders/) and the irony is that the solution to content burnout is using what you've already thought through, not generating new thoughts on demand. Your best insights are in your published posts. They're just sitting there, waiting to be reformatted.

## Tools that automate extraction

**[ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) or [Claude](https://claude.ai/)** — Paste any blog post and ask for social media variations. Specific prompts work better: "Write 5 tweets from this post. Each must be under 280 characters and standalone — no threads." I covered [Claude's new features](/posts/claude-45-sonnet-new-features-solo-builders/) if you want to use it effectively.

**[Opus Clip](https://www.opus.pro/)** — If you record a video version of your post, Opus Clip automatically finds the best 60-second segments and creates short-form clips. One video becomes 5–10 clips.

**[Descript](https://www.descript.com/)** — Transcribe your blog post as a video, then edit the transcript like a document. Remove filler, tighten sentences, export as a reel or podcast episode.

**[Notion](https://www.notion.so/)** — Build a simple content database: post title, publish date, social posts extracted, status. Check off each piece as you schedule it. I use Notion for [content planning](/posts/notion-ai-task-management-non-coders/) across multiple blogs.

**[Repurpose.io](https://repurpose.io/)** — Automatically distributes your video content across platforms. Record once, publish to YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and LinkedIn simultaneously.

## The bottom line

You don't have a content creation problem. You have a content distribution problem. The ideas are in your blog. The research is in your posts. The frameworks are in your archives. What's missing is the extraction workflow — and now you have one.

Start with your most popular blog post. Run it through the extraction process above. Schedule the pieces over the next week. Watch what happens. Then do it again with the next post.
