I spent three hours last month screening resumes for a freelance project manager. Forty applications, eight shortlisted, and I still couldn't tell which ones could hold a real conversation. That gap between what a resume says and what a person can actually do is exactly what a new wave of video-first AI hiring tools is trying to close.

Fika Jobs, based in Stockholm, raised $4 million in 2025 to build a platform where AI agents interview candidates on video before you ever see them. Instead of resumes, you browse short video profiles of people who've already been evaluated. The pitch sounds like TikTok for recruiting, and for solo builders who can't afford a bad hire, the model deserves a closer look.

I covered [how AI agents are changing the hiring process](/posts/fika-jobs-ai-video-hiring-solo-builders/) when the funding news dropped. But one startup isn't the story. Video-first AI hiring is becoming its own category, and if you're hiring your first contractor or employee, you need to understand the trade-offs before signing up.

## How does video-first AI hiring actually work?

Candidates connect their LinkedIn profile, and the platform's AI reviews their background. Then they complete a roughly ten-minute video interview with an AI agent — Fika uses Google's Gemini models for this. The platform clips the responses and organizes them into a browsable profile you can watch in a few minutes.

You're not reading cover letters or guessing whether a resume gap is a red flag. You're watching someone explain their experience in their own words, and the AI has already asked structured questions so the format stays consistent across every candidate.

Fika isn't the only player here. [Alex](https://www.alex.com/) raised $17 million to automate initial interviews, and [Mercor](https://mercor.com/) hit a $2 billion valuation with AI-powered recruiting. The difference is that most competitors focus on helping employers screen faster. Fika flips it — candidates maintain persistent video profiles that employers can browse anytime, not just when a role opens up. If you've been following [how AI agents are becoming employees](/posts/ai-agents-becoming-employees-solo-business/), this is the logical extension. AI isn't just doing the work; it's doing the hiring too.

## Why should solo builders care about AI video hiring?

When you're a one- or two-person operation, you don't have an HR department or a recruiting budget. You probably don't even have a job description template. You need someone who can do the thing, reliably, without you holding their hand.

Traditional hiring platforms are built for companies with dedicated talent teams. They hand you filters, assessments, and pipelines that all require time you don't have. Video-first AI hiring compresses that process. You watch three-minute clips instead of reading thirty resumes. You hear how someone communicates before you schedule a single call.

I've written about [building your first automation in 15 minutes](/posts/build-your-first-automation-in-15-minutes/) — hiring should be just as quick to set up. These platforms promise you can evaluate fit in a fraction of the time it normally takes.

Fika's pricing model is worth noting. It's free for job seekers, and employers pay nothing upfront. Fika takes a 10% cut of the candidate's first-year salary upon successful placement. For a solo builder hiring a contractor at $50/hour, that's a real cost. But if it saves you ten hours of screening and lands someone who actually fits, the math can work.

## What are the bias risks with video-based hiring?

Video profiles expose everything a resume hides — race, age, gender, appearance, accent, disability. There's a reason some companies moved to blind resume screening. When you can see someone before you evaluate their skills, unconscious bias creeps in whether you want it to or not.

Fika and similar platforms know this. Some are experimenting with AI-generated summaries that highlight skills and experience without showing the video first. But the core value proposition — see the person, not just the paper — is also the core risk.

If you're using these platforms, be deliberate. Watch videos with a scoring rubric in mind. Focus on what they say, not how they look. For roles where communication genuinely matters — sales, customer support, client-facing work — video profiles add real signal. For a backend developer? Stick with a technical assessment.

I covered some of this in [what happens when AI agents run your business](/posts/ai-agents-becoming-employees-solo-business/). AI can reduce friction, but it can also amplify existing biases if you're not paying attention.

## What should you look for in an AI hiring platform?

**Interview quality.** Does the AI ask structured, relevant questions, or is it reading a script? Fika's use of Gemini models for personalized questions based on LinkedIn profiles is a step above generic screening.

**Candidate experience.** A ten-minute video interview is reasonable. A forty-minute AI gauntlet will drive away good candidates.

**Pricing model.** Percentage-of-salary models work for full-time hires but get expensive for contractors. Look for platforms offering flat fees or per-posting pricing if you're hiring freelancers.

**Integration with your workflow.** Can you review candidates from your phone? Does it connect to tools you already use? Solo builders don't have time to learn another dashboard. If you've set up [AI to handle customer messages](/posts/ai-handle-customer-messages-solopreneur/), you want hiring to work the same way.

**Data privacy.** Video interviews contain biometric data. Check the platform's policies on retention, deletion, and who can access candidate videos before you commit.

## Is hiring becoming a content format?

Hiring is quietly becoming a content problem. Just like [AI tools changed how we create](/posts/build-a-tool-that-actually-does-something/), they're now changing how we evaluate people. Resumes are static documents. Video profiles are dynamic, searchable, and something AI can analyze at scale.

For solo builders, this cuts both ways. You can now access the same quality of candidate evaluation that used to require a recruiting firm. But if you're on the candidate side, your competition is now a polished video profile, not just a well-written resume.

The platforms launching today — Fika, Alex, Mercor, and others — are building infrastructure for a hiring process that looks nothing like what most of us grew up with. Whether that's progress or a problem depends on how carefully it's implemented.
